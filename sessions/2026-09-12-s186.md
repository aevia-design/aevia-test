### Legal pages benchmarked against Journi — `work/legal-pages/benchmark_v1.md`
**Done:** First benchmark of the S183 drafts against a real comparator. **Journi GmbH** (Vienna,
photo-book app, same product and jurisdiction, lawyer-drafted) plus myposter.
**Findings, ranked:**
1. **Biggest gap: AGB §7 has no non-defect list.** Journi enumerates what is NOT a defect —
   customer typos, wrong product selection, blank pages, insufficient photo quality. Our approval
   flow makes that line *clearer* than theirs, yet we never state it. This is the clause that
   prevents arguing about a free reprint.
2. **Liability: we omit third-party claims entirely** — the head most likely to bite (a customer
   sued over a photo they had no rights to upload). Journi also names the PHG; we say "if anyone
   is injured".
3. ⚠ **Our ODR omission is CORRECT but S183 recorded the wrong reason.** The real reason: the EU
   ODR platform was **permanently shut down 20 July 2025** (Reg. (EU) 2024/3228), data deleted.
   Journi still links it, so **their page is stale**. Recorded so nobody re-adds it after seeing
   it on a competitor site.
4. **Accessibility statement: we are exempt.** EAA applies to e-commerce since 28 June 2025 but
   carves out microenterprises (<10 staff AND <€2m turnover). Do not copy Journi's. ⚠ Not
   permanent — revisit if the business grows.
5. **Delivery: ours is better, keep it.** Journi calls its dates "binding"; ours are estimates.
6. **IP: a deliberate difference.** Journi takes a broad commercial licence over user content; we
   permit use only to design/produce/deliver/support. ⚠ **So we cannot show a real customer's book
   in marketing without asking.** Open owner decision, relevant to TO-DOS #21 (Instagram).
**Not benchmarked:** Journi's privacy policy **404s** at the linked path, so retention, their
processor list and AI disclosure went uncompared. Our 12mo/7yr figures stay a reasoned proposal.

### Liability clause worked through — `work/legal-pages/decision-liability.md`
**Decided:** **Keep the order-value cap. Do not exclude slight negligence.** Add the PHG by name
and third-party claims to the excluded heads.
**Why:** Journi excludes slight negligence entirely; on a €70–100 product the whole difference is
"refund nothing" vs "refund the book", bought by trading certain enforceability for a clause
attackable under §879 ABGB / KSchG — on the section where a struck clause drags its neighbours
down. The exposure that could reach a sole trader's private assets (injury, gross negligence,
intent, PHG) is **unexcludable either way**. B optimises what barely moves.
**Watch out for:** ⚠ **The decision is REVERSIBLE now and becomes irreversible at the FIRST LIVE
ORDER** — AGB §10 means each order locks in the version live when it was placed.
⚠ **Insurance is the real protection, not the clause.** Betriebshaftpflicht, a few hundred €/yr.
Owner considering. **Do not re-raise the Journi wording on "a competitor does it" grounds** — that
is exactly the argument rejected here.

### Owner review of the drafts — five corrections, one factual error found
**Decided by owner:** OpenAI stays disclosed (GDPR Art. 13 recipients). Cookies section stays
truthful-for-today with the dependency cross-linked rather than pre-written. Retention wording
follows the cheap mechanism, not the reverse.
**Found:** ⚠ **AGB §5 is factually wrong** — "You can request changes" implies a customer-driven
edit cycle that does not exist. Real flow: we design → we send a preview → customer requests
changes or approves → **they pay** → it prints. §3, §6 and §7 all depend on payment being the
point of no return and break together if one drifts. **Not yet fixed.**
**Retention:** the draft promises automatic deletion; **nothing deletes anything today** — no
lifecycle rule, no job in `functions/`, storage grows forever at 1–4 GB/order. Agreed fix: say
"12 months after **upload**" and set a 365-day GCS lifecycle rule. Age-based is all Object
Lifecycle can express; a scheduled function reading delivery dates is real code that fails quietly.
**Data-subject requests:** one month to respond (Art. 12(3)), extendable by two if notified within
the first month. Agreed mechanism: publish **`privacy@aevia.at`**, filter Gmail on *content*
(GDPR/DSGVO/Löschung/Auskunft/delete my data), keep a two-column received/answered log. A magic
subject line was rejected — real requests never use one. Also surfaced: **72h breach notification**
and **Art. 30 records** (the <250-employee exemption does not cover regular processing).
