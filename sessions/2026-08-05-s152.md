## 1. Why the spine had to change

Printsmarter specified **10mm at 40 pages and 14mm at 80 pages**; every template had `spine.wMm: 9`
hardcoded. Because the cover is one flat sheet (back | spine | front), a wrong spine width does not
just make the spine wrong — it **shifts the front artwork out of position by the same amount**.

It fails silently: no error, renders plausibly on screen, wrong only in the printed book.

**Scope is the cover only.** Inside pages are individual 200×200mm pages and the spine is not part
of their geometry. (Owner corrected an earlier overreach on this.)

## 2. The approach — and why not the obvious one

Recorded in `work/spine-geometry/brief.md`. Chosen via `/solutioning` over three alternatives.

**Coordinates are panel-relative, not absolute.** The key discovery is that cover CSV X values are
*already* panel centres — Scribble's front photo at `xMm: 327` is exactly `18 + 200 + 9 + 100`, the
front-panel centre in with-bleed space. And the `Type` column already declares the panel. So this
was arithmetic in the loader, not a bulk edit.

**Panel centres (with-bleed space), the single authority:**

| Panel | Centre | s=9 | s=10 | s=14 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Back  | `118`        | 118 | 118 | 118 |
| Spine | `218 + s/2`  | 222.5 | 223 | 225 |
| Front | `318 + s`    | 327 | 328 | 332 |

Front-panel items shift by `delta = s − referenceSpineMm`. Back items never move.

**Cover CSVs declare the spine width they were drawn at** (`referenceSpineMm: 9`). Chosen over
duplicating CSVs per book size (10 hand-synced files, no generator, no drift test) and over baking
a magic `9` into code. When Xenia later re-authors against the real 40pp book she changes one field.

**Spine caption X is KEPT, as an offset from spine centre (default 0).** The owner overruled a
proposal to remove it: different fonts at different sizes need visual centring. He was right — and
it strengthens the case, because under the absolute scheme any hand-tuned value silently breaks
when the spine changes and would need re-tuning per size.

**The SVG is split by panel, never stretched.** This is the load-bearing decision.

## 3. Why stretching was never an option

`renderCover()` positions photo slots as absolutely-positioned divs sized from millimetres; the SVG
is a separate overlay `<img>`. **They are independent.** Stretching the SVG across a wider sheet
moves the artwork *without* moving the photo beneath it — 1.22% at 80pp, which is **5mm** of drift
at the front panel's outer edge. Shaped openings (`clipShapes.coverFrame` in Newborn, Papercut,
Tender) would show it as a crescent at the ellipse edge.

**Implementation:** render the same SVG twice at natural width —
- back copy anchored left, clipped `0 → 200mm`;
- front copy clipped from `209mm`, translated right by `delta`;
- the spine band between them painted by the engine gradient / a flat sharp stripe.

At `delta = 0` the copies tile back into today's exact render.

**Consequence: Xenia re-authors nothing.** Re-authoring per size was considered and rejected — it
would need 2 SVGs, 2 clip paths and 2 `pxPerMm` values per template, and would *still* require the
coordinate shift because slots are data, not artwork. Two-way door if the render ever looks wrong.

## 4. What the cover SVGs actually contain (verified, not assumed)

- Authored as **named panel groups**: `<g data-name="Back">`, `"Spine"`, `"Front"`, plus `"LOGO"`.
- **Artwork does not cross the spine.** Newborn's `Back BG Color` rect ends at `566.929` user units
  = **exactly 200.0mm** (viewBox `0 0 1159.37 566.929` over 409mm → 2.8346 units/mm).
- **Bleed lives OUTSIDE the viewBox.** The rect starts at `x = −51.024` = −18mm, which is why
  `export-pdf.js` expands the viewBox before rendering. Panel splits must carry 18mm of bleed on
  the OUTER edges while cutting clean at the spine boundary.
- **The front panel has no background in the SVG** — the engine's canvas colour fills it. Back and
  spine backgrounds come from the SVG. This asymmetry is what makes the split natural.
- **The spine carries only CSV captions.** Owner: "we don't place our logo on the spine, never."
  The `LOGO` path sits at x ≈ 108.8 units ≈ 38mm — on the back panel.
- All six cover SVGs are **local**; `svgBase` is a relative path. Scribble's is under
  `assets/Template_Scribble/Spreads/Cover/`.

## 5. 🔴 The spine colour trap — check this in Phase 2

Since the split, the SVG's own spine strip falls in the gap between the two panels and is **not
drawn**. `sections.spine.bgColor` is now the *only* source of the spine colour.

**Tender's data disagreed with its artwork** — declared `#fbf8f6` (cream) while the SVG rect is
`#8a817a` (dark taupe). Harmless before (the SVG painted over it), visible now. Fixed to match the
artwork, and the owner mirrored it into the cover CSV's `bgColor` column.

Scribble was already correct (`#fdd16f` both). **Papercut, Newborn and Wander are unaudited.**

## 6. What was built and verified

**Engines** (`pages/staff/template-engine.html`, `pages/customer-preview.html`) — spine lookup
(40→10, 80→14, else 9), per-panel geometry, two-copy SVG split, spine-caption recentring,
`clipShape` x-translate corrected by `slotDeltaPx`.

**PDF** (`scripts/export-pdf.js`) — module-level cover constants made per-render; page count
threaded into `renderCoverImage()`, the cover page size and `drawCoverCaptions()`; the same
two-panel split implemented via sharp `extract` + `composite`; `clipShape` delta applied.
`services/pdf-renderer/index.js` needed **no change** — it only imports `generatePdfFromFirestore`.

**Verification, with fresh output:**
- `qa/verify-spine-s152.mjs` drives the staff engine on localhost and reads live DOM geometry.
  **34/34 checks pass** across Scribble and Tender at both page counts: canvas `400+s`, both SVG
  copies at natural 1227px (never scaled), back copy at 0, front copy at `delta*3`, slots and
  spine captions on their computed centres.
- Tender's `clipShape` x-translate measures **−702 at both 40pp and 80pp** — constant, proving the
  silhouette tracks both the slot and the shifted artwork. Without the fix it would drift 3px/15px.
- `tests/spine-geometry.test.js` (new, 19 cases) — `npm test`: **252 passed, 18 suites**.
- Owner confirmed visually: Tender's oval opening registers correctly at both page counts.

**Not verified:** the PDF compositing has **never executed**. It is proven by geometry assertions
only. The owner's first render is the first real run.

## 7. Delegated agents needed correction — twice, again

Same shape as S151. Both returned DONE; both had defects found by reading the diff.

**Engine agent:** (a) customer-preview read a `#page-count-select` element that **only exists in the
staff engine**, so for real customers the spine would have stayed 9mm forever; (b) it set the SVG
`width` to the widened canvas — i.e. it *stretched the artwork*, the exact thing the brief forbids.
Its own tests passed because they never measured the SVG against the slots.

**PDF agent:** composited the whole SVG at `left: 0` and painted a spine stripe over the seam,
leaving the front artwork unmoved while the photo slot shifted — reintroducing artwork/slot drift,
and disagreeing with the engines. Its comment even asserted "no separate front-panel compositing is
needed for Phase 1." It did get the per-render constants, page-count threading, clipShape delta and
19 tests right.

**Critic agent** (run on the brief) returned NO-GO; about half held. Valid: bleed under-specified,
clipShape remapping unaddressed, hardcoded `409` at `export-pdf.js:978`, browser/PDF divergence.
Invalid: claimed the cover SVGs "aren't stored locally" (all six are — it looked in the wrong
place) and mis-derived the caption offset by confusing the CSV's with-bleed and without-bleed
columns. **Verify every claim; a reviewer is strong at attacking a stated mechanism and unreliable
on facts it did not check.**

## 8. Print partner — context reviewed, call held separately

Elanders steered us to **Printsmarter** (same building; same production line + SiteFlow API
**assumed, unconfirmed** since S148). Printsmarter ≈ €10.42/book at 40pp with no volume commitment
vs Elanders ≈ €18 at our volume; crossover ≈ 20 books/day. Commercial position: **a higher price at
low volume is survivable; a volume commitment is not.**

**Overhang, hinge gap and turn-in were escalated to blocking risks, then closed by the owner** —
samples were already printed with Elanders on exactly this geometry (200mm cover height, 18mm bleed,
panels butted), produced in Illustrator before any engine existed, and came out correct. "Not
written down in the repo" was misread as "unknown". A printed sample is stronger evidence than a
document. **The spine is the only cover dimension that varies with page count.**

Only open print question: whether 10/14mm is a formula (`spine = 6 + 0.1 × pages` fits exactly,
≈0.2mm per leaf on 160gsm) or two rounded data points. Low risk either way — only two constants move.

## 9. Unrelated fix: PNG in the staff engine

The engine's 8 file inputs accepted `.jpg,.jpeg,.heic,.heif` — **no PNG** — while `order.html`
accepts `.png` from customers. A folder of 56 test files showed only 32 in the picker. Added `.png`
to all 8. Note the engine re-encodes through a canvas to JPEG q0.85 (`template-engine.html:1311`),
so PNG transparency flattens; pre-existing behaviour, applies to the customer path too.
