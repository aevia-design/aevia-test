### 01:00 — Legal research: two settled answers
**Done:** `work/legal-pages/brief.md` then `work/legal-pages/research_v1.md` (researcher-agent, sourced to WKO, EUR-Lex, CJEU)
**Decided:** Two questions closed with sources, do not re-derive them
**Why:** (1) **No cookie consent banner is needed today** — Firebase auth and Stripe are "strictly necessary" under the ePrivacy Directive and are consent-exempt. A scan of `home.html` and `order.html` found zero analytics or tracking scripts. (2) **A no-returns policy for personalised books is legally sound** — EU Consumer Rights Directive Art. 16(c) carves out "clearly personalised" goods from the 14-day withdrawal right, and CJEU C-205/21 (2022) confirms it applies regardless of whether production has started. This matched the owner's own expectation.
**Watch out for:** The banner exemption dies the moment analytics is added, and the owner intends to add it. The Datenschutz draft is already worded to anticipate that, so adding analytics needs a consent banner but **not** a policy rewrite.

### 01:50 — Photo retention: a proposal, not a fact
**Decided:** Proposed **12 months after delivery** for customer photos, 7 years for order and invoice records (Austrian tax law)
**Why:** There is no legal number for photo retention. GDPR Art. 5(1)(e) storage limitation is a principle, and 6 to 12 months is the defensible range: long enough for reprint requests, short enough not to hoard personal data. Owner asked directly whether 180 days was grounded — it was not, and the honest range replaced it.
**Watch out for:** ⚠ **Nothing deletes photos today.** There is no lifecycle rule and no deletion job anywhere in `functions/`. Storage grows forever at 1–4 GB per order. **Publishing a retention promise the system does not keep is worse than publishing nothing**, so the GCS Object Lifecycle rule must ship with, or before, the Datenschutz page.
