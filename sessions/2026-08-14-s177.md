### The shape of the work

The owner and Xenia had pre-produced German artwork (`-DE` / `_DE` suffixes) across every
template, mostly special pages. The ask: a customer picks DE or EN on the product page and
everything follows — mockups, order form, book artwork, AI captions, with staff seeing the
choice. Brief written with `/rageatc-core-oss:creating-briefs`, stage-gated at the owner's
request so he can eyeball each piece before the next is built:
**`docs/briefs/germanization.md`.**

**Owner's calls, settled:** one switch drives everything (no mixed EN form / DE book); the
choice is made on the **product page regardless of site half** — browsing `/de/` can still
order the English book; mockups re-shoot **functional pages only**; captions must be written
**natively in German, not translated**; print parity is a hard requirement; and the order form
gets a **string table, not a duplicated `de/order.html`** (the form is 3,381 lines and changes
often — a mirror would drift the way the page mirror already does).

**Architecture:** the language rides the **Heirloom monogram mechanism** — the proven per-order
variant pattern. No new machinery. One Firestore field, one function param, ≈€0.

### 2026-08-14 — The owner's H/V instinct was right, but only three-quarters right

**Decided:** the owner suspected the missing V (portrait) variants did not matter because H and
V share artwork, position coming from the CSV. He asked for validation rather than assent.
**Path-level comparison confirmed it for Toys and Steps in both templates** (identical paths,
≤0.07mm jitter) — one DE file serves both orientations, four exports avoided. **But all four Art
pages differ**: the outlined heading sits exactly 5mm higher in V, to clear the taller portrait
photo.

**Watch out for:** an instinct that is right in the general case can still have the exception
that matters. Checking cost minutes; assenting would have shipped crowded artwork.

### 2026-08-14 — Stages 2+3: one rule, three implementations

**Done:** `eea8e27` + `99d6748`. 37 `svgDe` overrides across ten data files; both engines
resolve through a shared `resolvePageSvg()`, `export-pdf.js` inlines the same expression in
`renderPage`, and the Cloud Run renderer puts `language` on the state it builds. **A page with
no DE artwork falls back to English**, so partial coverage degrades gracefully instead of
rendering blank.

`tests/de-artwork-resolution.test.js` (6 tests) locks the rule, asserts every `svgDe` resolves
on disk, and **mirror-checks all three surfaces** so print cannot drift from the approved
preview. **Confirmed red first** by breaking the engine call site and by pointing one path at a
missing file.

**Watch out for:** the resolution rule is duplicated by design (two parallel engines + a third
implementation in the exporter). The mirror test is what keeps them honest.

### 2026-08-14 — Papercut's V artwork, generated in-repo

**Done:** `99d6748`. Xenia was unavailable and the owner asked for a programmatic fix. Her two
new Art files are drawn at the **H** heading position; the V layout's 140mm photo top edge sits
at 30mm, and the H-positioned German heading ends at **30.68mm — a 0.68mm overlap**, on a spread
that draws artwork **above** photos. It would have printed.

`work/germanization/make-papercut-v-de.mjs` applies a **-14.174u (-5mm) group transform** — the
exact offset measured between her own EN H/V pair — as a `transform` attribute rather than
rewritten path data (identical result, no risk of misparsing curves). Verified by
`verify-v-shift.mjs`, which **measures the render, not the source**: rasterises each file, finds
the ink bounding box, and compares. German V heading lands at 14.67mm against English V's
14.33mm (the cap-height difference between KUNSTGALERIE and ART GALLERY); clearance goes from
**-0.68mm to +4.33mm**.

**Watch out for:** ⚠ **re-run `make-papercut-v-de.mjs` if those two DE files are ever
re-exported** — this is the in-repo-patch trap from S157.

### 2026-08-14 — The selector reused .chip, and the design-review agent ran blind again

**Done:** `9b8e198`. The first selector reused `.chip`, so a preference looked like a second
pricing decision — two 108px two-line boxes stacked, language sitting *above* the choice that
carries the price. Owner rejected it. Now: page count leads, language follows as a compact
segmented toggle (one 38px pill sharing a row with its label). Real `<button>`s with
`aria-pressed`, an `aria-label` on the group, an inset `:focus-visible` ring. One row at
1440/768/375/**320**, including the German page where "Buchsprache" + "DEUTSCH" are longest.

**Watch out for:** **`design-review` STILL has no browser access** — second session running. It
reviewed source instead of pixels and claimed the inactive-segment contrast was 4.61:1, not
5.61:1. **Mine was right**: its method omitted the gamma linearisation WCAG requires, and its
own stated shortcut would have produced 2.30:1, so it did not apply its formula consistently
either. Two of its findings *were* real (missing group `aria-label`, no focus style) and were
applied. See LEARNINGS.
