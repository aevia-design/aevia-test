### 13:00 — The low-res badge: AEV-094's red wall, and why the warning was useless

Owner's iPhone 17 photos, collected via an iCloud Shared Album, came back badged LOW RES.
Arithmetic explains it exactly: **a Shared Album caps the long edge at 2048, so a 4:3 photo
arrives 2048×1536 and misses our 1575px floor by 39 pixels — 2.5%.** At the pre-S40 threshold
of 1500 it would have passed.

Found the research rather than re-deriving it: `docs/briefs/order-flow-hardening.md:162-176`.
**1575 = a verified 200 DPI floor** on a 200mm page. Sound, and left alone.

**The owner's objection is what reframed the fix.** The copy said "may print soft if used
large" — but *Aevia's staff* decide how large a photo runs, so the customer cannot act on it.
**The only action available to them is to supply the original**, and for both real causes the
original still exists on the phone. Copy now names the cause (messaging apps, iCloud Shared
Albums) and the fix. Red badge → the amber already used by the cover-orientation warning.

**WhatsApp is worse than iCloud**: standard quality downsamples to ~1600px long edge, so ~1200
on the short side. Messaging covers Android too — **there is no Google Photos equivalent of the
Shared Album 2048 cap**, so do not invent one.

**Competitors are no help here and that is the finding:** Pic-Time shows this exact warning and
never says why a photo is small. Nobody in the sample explains the cause. Explaining it puts us
ahead, not level.

### 16:30 — Xenia's challenge on professional formats: RAW, TIFF, the cap

All three declined by the owner; full reasoning written into `docs/briefs/photo-formats.md`
under **"Settled S166 — do not re-raise"**. Headlines:

- **RAW carries no more detail than the JPEG beside it.** Same sensor. It carries tonal
  latitude, not resolution. And rendering one ourselves would override the photographer's grade.
- **TIFF's print path is already free** (`export-pdf.js` runs everything through sharp; pdf-lib
  embeds sharp's output). The browser is the gap, and size kills it: 150–250 MB per file.
- **The 40 MB cap costs €1–2 per order per year to lift.** Money was never the constraint.
  **`generateDerivative` has 1 GB / 120s** (`functions/index.js:1752`) and a 100 MB JPEG decodes
  to ~300 MB of raw pixels. **A failed derivative makes the browser fall back to the full-size
  original**, so lifting the cap alone would create the exact egress it appears to avoid.
