### 10:00 — The bug: 14 of 52 photos silently dropped from AEV-094

**Root cause:** web derivatives are written under the **original's filename**, so a HEIC
original produces `previews/photo_024.heic` containing **JPEG bytes** (`Content-Type:
image/jpeg` — confirmed in the bucket). The engine's `isHeicFile()` matched the `.heic`
extension *before* it ever sniffed the magic bytes, posted that JPEG to `convertHeic`, got
`input buffer is not a HEIC image`, retried three times, then returned `null` at
`template-engine.html:1502`. **No error was shown.** 52 uploaded − 14 HEIC = 38 displayed.
**Fix:** the bytes decide; name and MIME are consulted only when the bytes are unreadable.
Detection moved to `isHeicMagic()` in `photo-utils.js` so it can be unit-tested.
**Verified by the owner reloading AEV-094: all 52 photos present, nothing regenerated in GCS.**

**Watch out for:** the owner saw "50 objects" in the Cloud Console and reasonably concluded
two had failed to upload. **The console paginates at 50.** There were 52. Nothing was ever
lost at upload — the entire shortfall was downstream.

**Why it survived this long:** every previous test was from a Windows desktop, which sends
JPEG. The owner ordering from an iPhone was the first end-to-end exercise of the HEIC path.

### 11:30 — Competitor baseline, and the reframing it forced

`docs/briefs/photo-formats-competitor-baseline.md`. **The industry standard is exactly
JPG + PNG + HEIC** — Artifact Uprising, Journi, Papier, Mixbook and Shutterfly all accept
that set and nothing more (AU adds WebP; Blurb refuses HEIC). **No competitor accepts RAW**,
and the stated reason is the same as ours: browsers cannot display it.
**This reframed the whole task.** We were not behind on coverage — our picker already
offered the standard set. We were behind on *coherence*: six places each decided what
counted as a photo and they disagreed. **Owner verified Papier and AU verbatim; Journi
documents no formats at all**, so our copy only has to beat Papier's.

### 17:30 — Two Codex reviews, and the one that saved us from a worse bug

`gpt-5.6-sol`, read-only, on each commit.

**Review 1** killed a regression I had feared (a HEIC brand outside our list would now be
rejected) with a checkable argument: the server's own gate in `heic-decode/lib.js` accepts
**the identical six brands at the identical offset**. Client and server are exactly aligned —
**do not extend one without the other.** It also found that `img.onerror` resolved
`{w:0,h:0}` and `processOneFile` returned a photo object anyway, so **a photo the browser
cannot render counted as loaded** — which would have defeated the staff alert added an hour
earlier. Fixed; a 10s decode *timeout* is deliberately kept distinct and keeps the photo.

**Review 2 caught a customer-blocking regression I had just shipped.** The new policy judged
on filename extension alone, where the old code also accepted anything typed `image/*`.
**Android hands Chrome the provider's DISPLAY_NAME with no guarantee of an extension** — a
Drive/cloud pick can arrive named `image` with type `image/jpeg`. Those uploads would have
been refused. **Blocking a real customer is worse than the bug we set out to fix.** Fixed:
extension decides when known, otherwise an *exact* accepted MIME does (not any `image/*` —
that is what admitted WebP), and the file is stored under a canonical name because
`isImageFile()` also matches on extension. It also found my special-page error going to
`err-step2` (a *later* step, so **invisible**) and a filename interpolated into `innerHTML`.

**One Codex remedy rejected with reasons:** coupling `confirmUpload` to derivative success
would race against an async `onFinalize` trigger.

### 18:30 — A correction to my own claim

I had said "local sharp decoded `IMG_5249.HEIC` (4284×5712)". **Wrong** — that was
`.metadata()`, a header read. A real decode fails here: `No decoding plugin installed for
this compression format`; `sharp.format.heif.input.fileSuffix` lists only `.avif`.
**HEIC decode cannot be tested locally on Windows.** No conclusion changed — the proof the
*deployed* function decodes HEIC is direct (14 `previews/*.heic` exist with
`Content-Type: image/jpeg`) — but the evidence cited was wrong.
