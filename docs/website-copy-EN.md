### Process
*Шаги с римскими цифрами I–V.*

You bring the moments. We make the book.

Five steps, none of them design work.

I — **Choose your template.** Pick the one made for your occasion.

II — **Send your photos.** Upload them and add the details worth keeping: names, dates, first words, a few lines.

III — **We make your book.** Our team lays out every spread and typesets your words into the pages. Send photos on Sunday, open your full preview on Tuesday.

IV — **Approve, then pay.** Swap a photo, tweak a caption, give the final word. You pay only for a book you're happy with, nothing before.

V — **Receive it.** We print, bind, and ship it to your door.

You spend minutes. The book looks like it took weeks.

## About us

Meta title: About Us — Aevia

About us

The opposite of a blank screen.

Aevum. In Latin, a lifetime. An age.

Aevia began with our own photos. We wanted to turn them into something worth keeping, and every service we tried handed us a blank screen and a hundred decisions.

You choose a layout, pick a background, place each photo, size every caption. Hours later you have something that looks ok. Just ok. Most of us were never trained to design a page, and it shows.

We slowly realised the problem wasn't us. It was the endless choice. We make hundreds of small decisions every day, and few of them leave us happier. Sometimes you don't want more options. You want fewer, and you want to trust that whichever you pick will be good.

Think of a small kitchen with a short menu, every dish perfected: it beats a wall of options you have to second-guess.

So we built the opposite of a blank screen: a small studio in Vienna, a few templates, each designed with artists and illustrators we work with directly. You choose the one that fits your occasion, send your photos, and we compose the book by hand. You see the finished book, and pay only then.

What you're left with is the thing that mattered all along: a book you're proud of, made to be held and passed on. That is the idea behind our name. Aevum, a lifetime.

Fewer choices. A book worth keeping.

## Help / FAQ

Meta title: Help — Aevia

Questions & answers.

Everything you need to know about ordering and creating your book.

**How does ordering work?**

Choose your template, upload your photos and a few words. Within 48 hours you'll get your finished preview: the whole book, laid out by our team. Make the final touches if you like, swap a photo, tweak a caption, then approve. You pay only then, and your book goes to print.

**What if I don't like my preview?**

Then you don't pay. The preview is free and comes with no obligation. Before walking away, though: you can adjust it yourself, swap photos, rewrite captions, change the order. And if something feels off that you can't fix in the editor, just reply to your preview email and a real person will look at it.

**How do I pay?**

We accept all major credit and debit cards via Stripe. You're only charged after you approve your book, never upfront.

**How many photos do I need?**

It depends on the template and page count. We'll tell you the ideal range when you order. JPEG or RAW both work.

**What do printed photos actually look like?**

Printed photos will always look different from your phone screen. That's not a flaw, it's physics: screens emit light, paper reflects it. Colours will be slightly less vivid, and very dark or backlit photos may print darker than expected. Good rule of thumb: reduce your screen brightness to 50% to approximate how a photo will look in print.

For best results we recommend photos taken in good light, with the main subject filling most of the frame. Small faces in group shots, distant landscapes, or heavily filtered photos may lose sharpness or detail in print.

**Why do my photos look softer in the preview than I expect?**

While you review your book, the preview shows a lighter version of each photo so the pages load fast. Look closely or zoom in and photos can appear softer than the originals. That's expected, and it doesn't affect your printed book.

We print from the original, full-resolution files you uploaded, so your finished book looks sharper and more detailed than the screen preview. The only time a photo prints at lower quality is when the upload itself was low resolution: a screenshot, a heavily cropped image, or a photo sent over WhatsApp. If a photo is too low-resolution to print well, you'll see it flagged as soon as you upload it, so you can swap it right there before you finish your order.

**What quality should my photos be?**

Your phone camera is more than good enough: modern smartphones shoot at resolutions well above what printing requires. The main things to avoid: heavy Instagram filters, extreme crops, and very dark or strongly backlit shots. If you've edited your photos in an app, that's fine. Just avoid over-sharpening or heavy colour grading.

We print in the sRGB colour profile. Photos taken on a smartphone are automatically in sRGB. If you're uploading photos from a DSLR camera, check with your photographer that the colour profile is set correctly.

For best quality and correct photo ordering, upload directly from your phone or transfer via USB cable / AirDrop. Avoid WhatsApp or email; they reduce quality and remove date information.

**Do you deliver outside Austria?**

No, currently we deliver only in Austria. More countries coming soon. After you approve your preview, your book is printed and dispatched in 2 business days. Delivery takes 7 business days in Austria. Shipping costs are shown at checkout before you pay.

The art in your hands

Aevia began with one conviction: a photo book can be a piece of art, not a container for pictures. Something made, not assembled. So we work with artists. Every template is shaped by an illustrator or designer. On some, their work lives on the cover; on others, it runs through every spread. Every collaboration is its own; every book is unique.

We believe the work should be paid for. Our artists are compensated fairly for what they make, for as long as their work lives on in the books people order. Whether you are just beginning or long established, we would like to see what you make.

### The artists

**Kevin Lucbert** — Berlin & Paris — In collaboration on Wander

Kevin Lucbert was born in Paris in 1985 and works between Berlin and Paris. He graduated from the École Nationale Supérieure des Arts Décoratifs (ENSAD) in 2008. He co-founded the artists' collective The Ensaders with Yann Bagot and Nathanaël Mikles, and he takes part in performances and exhibitions and leads drawing workshops.

His clients include The New York Times, The New Yorker, Télérama, Les Échos, the Mondadori Group, Foscarini, Starbucks, Hermès, Hennessy, and BIC. BIC acquired several of his works for its contemporary art collection, shown at the Centquatre in Paris in 2018.

**Dorottya Juhász** — Vienna — In collaboration on Joyride

Dorottya Juhász was born and raised in Hungary and built her career in Budapest. She co-founded BEBI, a loungewear brand, and now lives in Vienna, where she works as creative director at the creative agency FranzJohann.

Her illustration style is unmistakably her own: scenes of everyday life, with women and their strength as a recurring theme. She has drawn for brands of every size, from ELLE to local places like the Twentysix restaurant in Budapest.
