_Deep-research pass, 2026-08-03 (S148). Purpose: stress-test the four core inputs of the Aevia
marketing model against **DACH- and premium-DTC-specific** evidence, not the US/global blends the
S111 brief relied on. Every claim is cited; full bibliography at the end._

**Product frame that governs every benchmark below:** Aevia is a **done-for-you, premium**
personalised photo book (~€70–100), sold as a **gift/occasion** purchase (newborn, anniversary,
memorial, milestone), launching in **Austria/DACH**, **organic-led** (Instagram/Pinterest). It is
**not** a DIY commodity tool like CEWE. Two consequences run through everything: (a) DACH — and
Germany especially — is the **most expensive** paid-social market in the EU [12]; (b) a €70+
considered *gift* to a brand nobody knows converts far **worse** than a blended e-commerce average.

## 1. Paid CPC — €0.85 is optimistic once you account for Instagram and Germany

The headline EU number appears to support €0.85: e-commerce advertisers on Facebook/Instagram in
Europe pay roughly **€9.50–12.50 CPM and €0.65–0.90 CPC** in 2026 [8]. Taken alone, €0.85 sits at
the top of that band — already not conservative.

But two product-specific facts push it higher:

- **You are Instagram/Pinterest-led, and Instagram is the expensive placement.** Instagram feed
  CPCs run **$1.83–3.35 versus Facebook's $1.06–1.72** [9], "reflecting a more engaged but premium
  audience." A visual gift brand naturally leans into exactly the pricier inventory. Instagram
  *Stories* are cheaper (~$1.83 CPC) [9], so placement mix matters — but an IG-led plan realistically
  sits well above €0.85.
- **Germany and Austria are among the most expensive paid-social markets in the EU.** The strongest
  evidence is now **Statista [A]**, not vendor blogs: Germany's Facebook **CPC ≈ $3.20** and Austria's
  Facebook e-commerce **CPM ≈ $9.24** (2024) [21][22]. Even discounting for objective/audience
  differences, $3.20 CPC for Germany is **nearly 4× the model's €0.85** — the clearest sign the input
  is too low. (The vendor-blog "€9.05 CPM, EU-highest" [C,12] now merely echoes what Statista shows.)

**Verdict: optimistic.** €0.85 is defensible only as an efficient-Stories floor; a realistic IG-led
DACH planning CPC is **€1.00–2.00+**, and Statista's Germany figure suggests it could run higher. CPC
alone doesn't decide the case — CAC does (§5) — but this input understates cost.

## 2. Conversion rate — 2%/3% is the weakest assumption, likely half of reality

This is where the model is most exposed. The "2%" instinct comes from citing a blended e-commerce
average, but almost none of that average is *cold paid traffic to a €70+ unknown gift*:

- **Cold paid social converts at ~1.1%** because the channel's job is discovery, not conversion [3].
  The full-year 2025 **Meta median is 1.6%** [11] — and that already includes warmer, retargeted
  audiences, so cold sits below it.
- **High AOV drags conversion down hard.** Every store converting above 4% in one 21-store dataset
  had an AOV **under $80**; the analysts warn that a $200 store converting at 1% may be
  *outperforming* [4]. Aevia's €70–100 sits in the penalised zone.
- **Your core segment is the worst-converting one.** Baby & child e-commerce converts at
  **0.49–0.65%** [5][6] — a quarter of the blended average — because baby purchases carry a long
  consideration period. Newborn is a headline Aevia occasion.
- **The German 2.22% average is a red herring** [5]. It's all-traffic (search + returning + branded
  + direct), not cold paid. Using it to justify a paid-acquisition CVR compares unlike with unlike.

**Verdict: very optimistic — roughly 2× too high.** A realistic cold-paid CVR for a premium gift
in DACH is **0.7–1.3%**. The 3% "stretch" is not a stretch, it's a fantasy for cold paid traffic;
3% belongs only to warm/email/returning audiences [3]. **Leave the base at ~1% for planning and let
the promo test set the real number** — this single change is the most important correction in the model.

## 3. Referral share — broadly right, mildly optimistic on how fast it matures

Here the model holds up well. Mature refer-a-friend programmes drive **5–10% of revenue at the
median, up to ~30% at the top quartile** [7][14], and referral is repeatedly the *cheapest* channel
at **€15–50 CAC** [7] — which comfortably justifies your €10 reward. Your ramp (3–5% early → 8–10%
mature) is inside the evidence.

The only caution is **timing**: reaching 8–10% requires a sizeable installed base *and* an actively
promoted programme; brands don't arrive there in year one. Referral share is also a % of *total*
orders in your model, so it rides on the acquisition engine actually producing customers to refer.

**Verdict: reasonable.** Keep it, but treat the 8–10% "mature" figure as 2027+ and don't let it do
heavy lifting in the launch quarters.

## 4. Repeat rate — fair, but the number hides an occasion-mix risk

The 20%/18-month figure is defensible against general DTC data: only **18.8% of customers place a
second order within 365 days** across 156k DTC customers [10]; the oft-quoted average is **25–30%**
[13]; **luxury sits at ~10%** [13]. Over an 18-month window, 20% is a sensible middle.

The photobook category looks *better* — market data cites ~**42% annual repeat and 1–2 orders per
active customer per year** [15], because habitual photobook makers document each new trip/event. But
that describes **DIY commodity customers** (CEWE-style), not done-for-you *gift* buyers. Aevia's
repeat depends entirely on **which occasion**:

- **Memorial → effectively one-off** (a customer rarely repeats a memorial book).
- **Newborn / anniversary / milestone → genuinely repeatable** (new babies, each year, each event) —
  and personalised post-purchase flows lift second-purchase rates materially [10][17].

**Verdict: reasonable, but occasion-mix-sensitive.** If your early mix skews to one-off occasions
(memorial), real repeat could fall to **<15%**; if it skews to newborn/anniversary and you run
occasion-tagged lifecycle marketing, 20%+ is achievable, even conservative. Model it as a range, and
tag every order by occasion from day one so you can measure it per-segment.

## 5. The real DACH CAC band — the number the model most needs

Three sources of differing quality point the same way — up:

- **Statista [A]:** German Facebook CPC ~$3.20 and Austrian Facebook e-commerce CPM ~$9.24 (2024)
  [21][22] — the raw media cost is high before any conversion loss.
- **Shopify Global Commerce Report [A]:** CAC is **up ~40% since 2023**, spanning **$53–377 by
  category** across 4.8m merchants [24]. Premium/considered categories sit in the upper half.
- **Vendor blogs [C]:** German DTC lifestyle blended CAC €60–110 [12]; premium/luxury >$175 [16].
  Now used only as *corroboration* of the primary sources, not as the load-bearing figure.

Your model's implied paid CAC is **€42.5** (€0.85 ÷ 2%). Every source above sits well beyond it. But
the honest position is a **range, not a point:** desk research cannot produce a trustworthy
premium-gifting-in-Austria CAC, so treat the realistic paid CAC as **~€70–150** pending the promo
test. _(The first draft claimed a precise ~€85 by "convergence"; that is withdrawn — both legs came
from the same vendor.)_

**Implication for the business case — unchanged and now better-sourced:** at a paid CAC anywhere in
€70–150 against ~€46 gross profit on a €70 book, **each paid order loses money before fixed costs**.
Paid can still play a role — retargeting warm traffic, seasonal Q4 gifting spikes, and *measuring*
CAC — but it cannot be the growth engine. Organic (≈€0) and referral (~€10–50 [C,7]) are the only
channels whose unit economics work at €70.

---

## Synthesis — what to change in the model

1. **Conversion → ~1% base** (from 2%), 3% deleted as a cold-paid figure. Biggest single correction.
2. **CPC → €1.00–1.20** planning figure if IG-led (from €0.85); or better, **drive the paid engine
   off a measured CAC** and treat CPC×CVR as a cross-check only.
3. **Expect paid CAC ≈ €70–150** (range, not point), not €42.5 — so the model's early, paid-leaning
   quarters are optimistic on both order count and spend efficiency. Re-read the runway with this.
4. **Referral and repeat stay**, with referral's mature share pushed to 2027+ and repeat modelled as
   an occasion-mix-sensitive range (tag orders by occasion).
5. **The strategic conclusion is reinforced, not overturned:** at €70, paid is loss-making per order;
   growth has to come from organic scale and a price that rises with trust.

## Limitations & caveats

- **Some Austria-specific data now exists but sits behind paywalls.** Statista carries Austria/Germany
  Meta CPM/CPC series [21][22] and the Handelsverband/KMU Forschung *eCommerce-Studie Österreich* [25]
  is the authoritative Austrian source — but full figures require paid access. The values cited here
  come from the publicly visible summaries; buying the Handelsverband study would give the strongest
  Austrian grounding for a funding pitch.
- **Conversion is deliberately test-only.** No credible source gives a cold-paid CVR for premium
  gifting in Austria — it does not exist publicly. The promo test is its sole ground truth; do not
  present a desk-researched conversion number to an investor.
- **CAC is a range, not a point.** €70–150 spans the credible sources; precision requires the test.
- **Source tiers are marked inline** ([A]/[B]/[C]). No conclusion now rests solely on a [C] vendor
  blog; the load-bearing numbers (CPC, CAC direction) are anchored to [A] Statista/Shopify.
- **These remain pre-launch estimates** — honest *priors*, not forecasts.
