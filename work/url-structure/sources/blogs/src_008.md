# How Long Should You Keep a Redirected (301) URL Before Shutting It Down?

Source: https://www.highervisibility.com/seo/learn/how-long-keep-301-redirect-before-removing/
Publisher: HigherVisibility (SEO agency blog)
Retrieved: 2026-09-23 via WebFetch (summarized extract)

## Minimum Timeframe

Maintain 301 redirects for at least one year, giving Google enough time to recognise the page migration and transfer ranking signals: "you should keep your 301 redirects in place for at least a year to give Google enough time to process the page move."

## Why Removing Too Soon Is Risky

- **Traffic loss**: external sites may still link to the old URL; removing the redirect creates dead links and eliminates referral traffic.
- **Ranking impact**: active inbound links to redirected pages continue driving value to the new URL; removing the redirect severs this benefit.
- **User experience**: visitors following outdated links hit 404s instead of content, harming brand perception.

## Best Practice

"If you see no pressing reason to remove a 301 redirect, it's probably just best to leave it there." Keep redirects active as long as external links point to the old URL, or indefinitely if feasible.
