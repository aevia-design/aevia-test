Five ways to put Aevia on the envelope

Same stock, same format, same fastening. What changes in each study is how much is said and how loudly — from a single blind-debossed mark to a printed letter across the flap.

One rule runs through all five: deboss carries the large forms, print carries the small text. A die pressed into coloured stock loses fine detail, so the logo is debossed and every line below roughly 12 pt is printed instead — which the supplier can do in any colour on any of their stocks.

Everything is drawn at true scale: 1 mm on your screen is 1 mm on the envelope. Every element carries its real millimetre size, so a chosen study can go straight into Illustrator without re-guessing anything.

Study A

Quiet Mark

The rumi reference, read literally. One blind-debossed lockup low on the face, one small printed line high on the flap, and nothing else. The envelope stays an object; the brand is something you notice on the second look rather than the first.

Lockup
70 mm wide, centred, 146 mm from top
Treatment
Blind deboss, no ink
Flap line
7 pt caps, 0.22 em tracking, gold foil or flat print
Colours
Stock + one print colour

Where this breaksThe mark's thin book outline is roughly 0.4 mm of stroke at this size. If your supplier's minimum is 0.5 mm, the frame fills in and reads as a blob. Test the mark alone before committing.

Keep your memories beautifully
Study B

Full Measure

The Midge Maisel move: the wordmark goes nearly edge to edge along the bottom, big enough that the deboss becomes the surface rather than a detail in it. Confident, and the safest of the five in production — large forms hold a clean edge on textured stock where small ones don't.

Lockup
180 mm wide, 20 mm side margins
Baseline
Sits 8 mm above the bottom edge
Tagline
7.5 pt caps above, printed
Colours
Stock + one print colour for the tagline

Where this breaksAt 180 mm the lockup is bigger than most deboss dies. Ask for the maximum die size before you fall in love with it; above roughly 150 mm this often becomes a two-hit job or a price jump. This is the one study where the size limit, not legibility, is the risk.

Inside is a year you already lived,
set down so it stops slipping away.

in Vienna, for you.
Study C

The Letter

Reference 1, where the flap carries a short piece of writing and the brand barely signs it. This is the only study that says something to the customer instead of naming itself, which suits a product whose whole promise is words and pictures kept properly. The mark alone sits low, like a maker's stamp.

Text block
144 mm wide, centred, 4 lines
Type
7.5 pt serif, 1.75 line-height
Treatment
Printed, as reference 1 does it
Mark
16 mm, debossed, centred, 160 mm from top

Where this breaksPrinting solves the legibility, so the remaining risk is the writing. Four lines on a flap have to survive being read hundreds of times, in two languages, by people in very different moods — the copy shown is a placeholder to judge the shape of the block, not a proposal.

Study D

The Mark

No name, no address, no tagline. One 44 mm book glyph debossed into the lower face and the stock left to do everything else. The most disciplined option and the cheapest to make — a single small die, one hit, no print run. It also asks the most of the mark, which has to carry recognition alone.

Mark
44 mm wide, centred, 140 mm from top
Stroke
≈ 1.6 mm at this size — comfortably above any minimum
Treatment
Blind deboss, single die
Colours
Stock only

Where this breaksNothing on the envelope says Aevia to someone who has not seen the mark before — including the postman. If these ever ship unboxed rather than inside an outer carton, you need the name somewhere.

Study E

Ledger

The editorial option, and the only one that uses the envelope's full height. Wordmark and a hairline rule sit at the top like a masthead; the founding line and the URL anchor the bottom corners. It reads as a document rather than a package, which is closer to how the books themselves are set.

Lockup
52 mm wide, 22 mm from left and top
Rule
176 mm × 0.35 mm, printed, 45 mm from top
Footline
7 pt caps printed, both corners, 196 mm from top
Colours
Debossed logo + one print colour

Where this breaksPrinting the rule removes the production problem and leaves a compositional one: a line that long only works if it is dead parallel to the top edge and the flap fold. Any drift in trimming shows up as a rule that looks crooked even when it is straight.

What I need from you before this becomes artwork

  1. A vector logo. The only Aevia logo in the repo is a 409 × 101 px PNG. A deboss die is cut from vector outlines, so the supplier cannot use it. Everything above is that PNG masked into shape — good enough to judge a layout, not good enough to make one. This is the hard blocker.
  2. The supplier's deboss minimums — smallest stroke width, smallest type size, and maximum die dimensions. Those three numbers decide between A, B and D on their own.
  3. Which print colour, and on which stock. Printing in any colour is confirmed available, so this becomes a taste decision rather than a constraint. Shown here in the brand's warm gold (#9C7B4E) on mauve; a tone-on-tone ink a few shades darker than the stock would read quieter, and white would read sharper. Worth printing all three on paper before choosing.
  4. The real envelope size and flap depth. Drawn here at 220 × 220 mm with a 76 mm flap, taken from the template proportions. If it turns out to be 225 mm, every coordinate shifts.
  5. The stock colours actually on offer. One mauve is shown; the layouts do not change with colour, but your choice of which studies to test might.
  6. A decision on the tagline. Studies B and C use "Keep your memories beautifully" from the style guide rather than the "a life in pages" idea, which is not settled. Whichever wins needs a German version, since the books now ship in both languages.
