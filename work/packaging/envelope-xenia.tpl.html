Before this goes to the supplier

Two findings and four open questions

The small text cannot be debossed Xenia's render shows every element tone-on-tone, which reads beautifully on screen. On paper a die pressed into coloured stock loses fine detail, so anything under roughly 12 pt fills in. That is the paragraph and both corner lines. Setting Small text → Printed shows the honest version: the lockup pressed, the small text printed. It is the same split the supplier's own references use — reference 1's paragraph is printed, not pressed.
The lockup is real vector now This page draws logotype 1 - aevia - vector.svg directly rather than masking the old PNG, so the blocker that stopped S179 is gone. One number to carry forward: the mark's inner square is a 5.2 unit stroke on a 408 unit width, which at 46 mm wide comes out near 0.6 mm. That is thin for a die and is the first thing to check against the supplier's minimum.
Still unanswered
  • Deboss or emboss. The Relief toggle shows both. Pressed in reads quieter and is the safer bet on a soft coloured stock; raised out catches more light and shows the lockup off, but it stresses the fibre and can crack on a heavy die. Worth asking the supplier which they would rather run on this paper.
  • Relief minimums. Smallest stroke, smallest type, maximum die size. The 0.6 mm stroke above rests on all three, and an emboss usually needs a heavier minimum than a deboss.
  • Confirm 220 × 220 mm and the 76 mm flap. Still read off the template's proportions, never stated by the supplier. At 225 mm every coordinate here shifts.
  • Which print colour. Tone-on-tone, white and gold are all previewed above, and all three read differently on paper than on a screen.
  • A German twin for the paragraph. The books ship in both languages now. Five lines of English on the envelope means five lines of German exist, or the envelope is English-only by decision rather than by default.
